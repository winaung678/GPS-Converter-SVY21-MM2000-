Best Practices for Using Smartphone GPS in Field Reconnaissance

When it comes to establishing legal property boundaries or pouring concrete foundations, professional surveyors rely on Real-Time Kinematic (RTK) GPS rovers and Total Stations to achieve millimeter accuracy. However, carrying this heavy and expensive equipment is not always practical for every stage of a project.

The Role of Smartphones

Modern smartphones are equipped with increasingly powerful GNSS (Global Navigation Satellite System) chips. While they cannot replace RTK rovers for final layouts, smartphones have become an invaluable tool for preliminary site reconnaissance, asset tracking, and rough staking.

Maximizing Accuracy

To get the best results from a smartphone GPS, field workers should employ specific techniques:

  • Averaging: GPS signals fluctuate due to atmospheric interference. By recording a point over a 10 to 30-second interval and calculating the mathematical average of the coordinates, users can significantly reduce the margin of error.
  • Geoid Application: As discussed in previous articles, raw GPS altitude is unreliable. Using software that applies EGM2008 or EGM96 geoid models on the fly ensures the elevation data is tied to Mean Sea Level.

Photographic Evidence

One distinct advantage a smartphone has over a traditional survey rover is its high-definition camera. By utilizing specialized web applications, surveyors can take field photographs that automatically embed GPS coordinates, local datum information, and timestamps directly onto the image. This watermarked visual evidence is highly beneficial for daily progress reports and client communications.
